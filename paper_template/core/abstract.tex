\section*{Abstract}
Despite its recent rise in popularity, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) has been the prevailing approach to grounding large language models (LLMs) in external information, and traditional RAG pipelines are still shallow: they retrieve, concatenate, and generate without modelling the consequences of clinical decisions, without calibrated uncertainty, or without faithful explanations of which evidence led to an answer. We introduce a twelvestage framework that extends RAG by incorporating three capabilities that are key for clinical question answering: (i) an action conditioned latent world model for simulating patient state evolution and enabling deliberate, tree-of-thought plan search; (ii) a deep-ensemble predictor that outputs calibrated diagnostic uncertainty with explicit decomposition into epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty; and (iii) a Shapley-value attribution module for quantifying the contribution of each retrieved document. The neural core (causal graph-attention encoder, gated recurrent latent dynamics model with supervised reward head and a fivemember probabilistic ensemble) has a total of only 10.1M parameters and can be trained within 20 minutes on a single consumer GPU. The world model achieves a nextstate mean absolute error of 0.039 and an expected calibration error of 0.029 on the DDXPlus+MedMCQA+MedQA corpus (100,000 samples). This thesis enhances the clinical concept coverage from 0.70 to 0.97, answer-structure completeness from 0.50 to 0.84 and evidence grounding from 2.0 to 5.1 citations per answer over a ten complex, clinical vignettes test set, and also introduces calibrated confidence, treatmentplan simulation, and per-document explanations, which the baseline is unable to offer. All code, setup and figures are published to enable replications.
